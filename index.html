Background

I am a mission-driven, not field-driven researcher. When I came to Stanford, I studied biology and did research in computational genomics because of the hope that next-generation sequencing would transform medicine. After realizing that much of molecular biology hinges on a small set of chemical reagents, I added a chemistry major (and a physics minor) and joined a lab synthesizing fluorescent molecules for nucleic acid assays. Two years ago, when I came across the terrible heart disease ATTR, my initial reaction was to use the chemistry I knew to create molecular therapies in Stanford's CHEM-H Undergraduate Entrepreneurship Program. But when I realized the real bottleneck was diagnosis, not treatment, I joined Stanford's AI Lab, tacked on a computer science major (and a math minor), learned everything I could about vision transformers, and developed first-in-class post-trained DINOv2 models for early diagnosis using SPECT scans. I then applied my knowledge of vision transformers to building end-to-end robotics stacks for laboratory environments, using tools like IsaacSim, Gazebo, and MoveIt2 to build digital twins and post-train open-source VLMs, VLAs, and World Models such as COSMOS-3 to autonomously operate a High-Performance Liquid Chromatography and NanoTemper machine.

Projects

Machine learning · Medical imaging

Vision Transformers for ATTR Classification

A comparison of a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone with a trained classification head, a custom CNN, and radiomics-based logistic regression for distinguishing controls, ATTR cardiomyopathy, and metastatic bone cancer. Evaluated on 1,000 synthetic whole-body bone scintigraphy scans, with attribution maps to examine model decisions. The ViT performed best on the held-out synthetic test set; clinical validation remains future work.

Cheminformatics · Predictive modeling

Structure to Affinity

Predicting human GSK-3β binding affinity from molecular descriptors and Morgan fingerprints using BindingDB measurements. The analysis compares ridge regression and random forests on 115 unique molecules, using scaffold-disjoint validation to test generalization to new chemistry. A fingerprint random forest modestly improves the holdout error over a median baseline, while bootstrap uncertainty and a random-split comparison highlight the limits of the result.

Chemical biology · Kool Lab

Coumarin–Imidazole Photocages for RNA

Developing conjugation-extended coumarin scaffolds toward longer-wavelength, light-controlled RNA chemistry. The project designs three coumarin–imidazole candidates and explores a synthetic route through C3 bromination, Heck coupling, pyridine methylation, and imidazole-carbonate formation. NMR characterization documents intermediate synthesis, with RNA caging and light-triggered release as next steps.

Single-cell genomics · Tan Lab

Perturb-C & FFPE Workflows for 3D Genomics

Developing experimental workflows to connect genetic and chemical perturbations with single-cell genome organization. The work investigates guide-region PCR compatibility with Dip-C preparation, amplification at low DNA inputs, and nuclei isolation from archived FFPE tissue. These preliminary assays support future perturbation screens and studies of aging-associated changes in the brain.
